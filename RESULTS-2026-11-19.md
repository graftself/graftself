# First results, 2026-11-19

## Training run full2
- Base: Qwen3.8-27B in bf16, LoRA on all layers, trained on an M5 Mac (128 GB) with MLX.
- 9,167 steps over two epochs of my own sent emails, texts and GitHub writing, packed into 1,024-token rows.
- Held-out loss went from 2.20 at step 500 to a best of 2.081 at step 8,000, then flattened.
- Gated DeltaNet layers trained with a chunked kernel (see mlx-gdn-train), about 140 tokens a second, about 60 GB.
- Canary secrets were planted in the data to measure memorization later.

## Blind test: 30 real customer and supplier messages
Each item: my real reply vs the model's reply, shuffled. A separate judge reads both without knowing
which is which, picks the better one, and lists any fact not backed by the message.

| Version | Picked or tied (of 30) | Replies with an unbacked fact | Contradicts looked-up facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| v2: tuned, no lookups | 12 | 24 | n/a |
| v3: tuned + lookups | 11 | 25 | 4 |
| Base model + lookups + 5 example replies | 11 | 18 | 1 |

Pass bar: picked or tied on 20 of 30.

## What it means
The voice transfers: casing, sign-off and length match. Facts do not. A model trained only on old
replies learns to answer confidently without reading anything, so it invents prices and claims
actions ("I just sent a label") and even overrides a correct stock status it was handed.
The judge also flags 21 of my own 30 real replies, because I know things that are not in the
message. So the honest fact metric is the code-counted contradiction rate, not the judge's.
